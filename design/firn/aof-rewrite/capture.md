Decision: Capture reserves R = floor(maxmemory / 20), or 16777216 bytes when maxmemory is zero, and admits only with R plus max(R, 1048576) bytes of service headroom, because firn-q-rw-limits A selects a five-percent service-first experiment with an explicit unlimited-memory budget, instead of retaining an unbounded after-image log or excluding rewrite storage from maxmemory. Budget exhaustion aborts capture, leaves client commands and ordinary AOF writes intact, reports aof_last_bgrewrite_status:err, and disables automatic retry until a manual attempt succeeds ([resource accounting](../../../research/investigations/aof-rewrite/scan-log.md#stage-2-implementation)).

Decision: Per-statement scratch coalesces complete owned replacement images before publishing one sequence-and-length-framed unit into fixed 64-KiB journal chunks, because an EXEC or script must commit its final images with its normal effects and Meta counter while cleanup must release bounded owners, instead of borrowed entry descendants, a second exported keyspace, or recursively releasing an entire journal under Meta. Capture stops at 1024 payload visits or 64 KiB of measured serialized work per statement; oversized values continue to work for clients but abort the rewrite.

Decision: Each live scan step uses map_scan count hint 1 and immediately charges the returned KeySet before copying payloads, as the owner's stage-2 addendum permits pending [firn-gap-scan-bound](https://claude.ai/artifact/7tocXS3iUdthCLCQCMd3ip), instead of blocking every other part of stage 2 on strict preallocation charging. This is a known limitation, not the approved strict reserve bound: a one-key step can exceed R by that key's allocation. Inspection of the pinned implementation also shows that colliding keys can share a step even with hint 1, so the general limit is one returned step's storage, not provably one key; the requested clarification remains open. The exact-reserve long-key case stays ignored only for that recorded gap ([pinned evidence and addendum](../../../research/investigations/aof-rewrite/scan-log.md#stage-2-implementation)).

Decision: BGREWRITEAOF answers started after S0 admission, because the rewrite is asynchronous and firn-q-rw-limits A selects later failure reporting through INFO, instead of delaying the reply until rotation. A rewrite remains in progress until its worker and retained journal chunks have been released; a new attempt cannot reuse their reserve early. Service pressure aborts retained work before installation commits under Meta; after that commitment eviction waits for publication and cleanup, because the manifest publication must finish with one authoritative outcome just as it must under shutdown.

Decision: The firn-only INFO fields firn_aof_rewrite_reserve and firn_aof_rewrite_scan_peak expose the admitted allowance and the largest observed PRE-2 heap increase across KeySet creation and one map_scan call, because the ignored long-key regression needs allocation evidence independent of the reservation ledger under the reported-facts rule, instead of testing accounting against itself. The field firn_aof_rewrite_cut reports whether the latest admitted attempt actually crossed S1, because the backlog regression must distinguish post-cut buffer growth from scan-journal exhaustion. The scan-peak field is a process-wide before/after sample, not total rewrite peak or RSS; the regression uses one quiescent key and checks the necessary bound that even one step must fit R. Concurrent unrelated allocation or release can affect the sample, so it is not a general exact-reserve proof.

Decision: Collecting-buffer growth while the sole writer seals and rotates also consumes R, using a separate statement-local LogBudget committed with the mutation under Meta, because maxmemory excludes ordinary AOF capacity and constant-sized live values can otherwise grow an unbounded post-S1 backlog, instead of treating reuse of the two buffer owners as a capacity bound. The encoder charges full new backing capacity before growth, including overlap; an overflow marks capture failed before the ordinary append proceeds, and the boundary reader observes it between chunks and falls back to the old file. Private command-effects buffers and non-command byte builders remain ordinary allocations; only their final collecting-buffer append uses this rotation budget.

Rejected:
- Automatic retry after capture abort: rejected because reserve or value-size pressure can persist and repeated attempts would consume service headroom without owner action.
- Splitting a large unversioned value over separate observations: rejected because writes between observations could produce a value that never existed.
- Treating post-allocation KeySet charging as strict reserve enforcement: rejected because the allocation has already happened; firn-gap-scan-bound remains required for that guarantee.
