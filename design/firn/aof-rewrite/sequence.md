Decision: The reconciled-scan experiment uses one u64 last-write stamp inline in each entry and one u64 commit sequence for an entire mutating atomic statement, taken lazily and shared by all of its command parts, because the owner's ruling A on [firn-q-rw-stamp](https://claude.ai/artifact/7tocXS3iUdthCLCQCMd3ip) selects coherent entry observations and multi-key execution-unit ordering for the first comparison, instead of a parallel stamp map or a sequence per key or AOF record ([sequence contract](../../../research/investigations/aof-rewrite/scan-log.md#sequence-and-mutation-coverage)). Access metadata refreshes are not dataset writes; failed and no-effect commands take no sequence unless they remove expired data. Startup and loading leave the counter and stamps zero, and exhaustion saturates the counter and refuses further captures without refusing service changes.

Decision: FLUSHALL and FLUSHDB advance the statement sequence and abort an active reconciled scan, because the owner's ruling A on firn-q-rw-stamp avoids enumeration of a cleared dataset and reset-aware reconciliation in the first comparison, instead of retaining a database-reset image. A flush inside EXEC or a script shares its outer statement sequence.

Rejected:
- A parallel stamp map: rejected because it retains duplicate keys and capacity and adds lookups and atomic targets to the same entry observation.
- A sequence per key or propagated AOF record: rejected because one EXEC or script can change many keys and append several records while remaining one indivisible execution unit.
- Omitting entry stamps in the first comparison: rejected because the owner selected inline stamps for observation and auditing; their production cost remains subject to the investigation's inactive-cost comparison, and ordered replacement emission alone does not require them.
- Wrapping the sequence counter: rejected because a later mutation would appear earlier than observations retained by a capture.
