Decision: A connection keeps Firn's existing receive/execute/send owner until its first successful subscription, then transfers once to independent receive, command and output owners, because unsolicited delivery and EOF must proceed while commands or socket sends wait and never-subscribed GET/SET must retain their existing shared targets, instead of splitting every connection at acceptance ([ownership and delivery](../../research/investigations/firn/connection-waits.md#ownership-and-delivery)).

Decision: Membership transitions and complete reply groups commit under one event hub, and one non-network dispatcher publishes committed frames in order to independent per-connection output inboxes, because publication order must survive wake races while a slow socket cannot delay other subscribers, instead of publishers sending directly or racing to append to recipient inboxes after releasing the registry hold. Immutable publication bodies are retained once and shared by recipient frames; the stream has one output owner, including its partial-send offset ([atomic publication](../../research/investigations/firn/connection-waits.md#atomic-publication-not-a-wake-race)).

Decision: A promoted connection charges the reserved byte capacities of each outstanding frame's prefix and body until that complete frame is released, charging a shared body in full to every recipient for its output limit, because a wire-length counter can miss retained spare capacity and must not lose the charge when ownership moves through hub, dispatcher, inbox and socket owner, instead of charging only enqueued wire bytes or releasing charges on dispatch. Active pub/sub clients use a hard threshold of at least 32 MiB and a soft threshold of at least 8 MiB continuously for more than 60 observed Unix seconds; normal clients have disabled output limits. Allocator rounding, queue storage and reference-control overhead are not represented by this charge, so closure at an identical publication count to Redis is not promised ([accounting and remaining evidence](../../research/experiments/pubsub/README.md#limits-and-remaining-evidence)).

Decision: Receive/send failure and pressure removal invalidate membership before cancellation wakes the independent owners, while QUIT and protocol errors commit an ordered finish marker after their reply, because cancellation, accepted command effects, reply delivery and child completion are distinct boundaries, instead of rolling back a completed command on a network error or joining a child before enabling its cleanup. A promoted command begins at its live-registration and cancellation admission check; invalidation prevents later admission while an already admitted command may finish, because queued cancellation can lag registry removal. Query bytes remain charged across both the receiver queue and parser's incomplete input, and closure occurs above one GiB; subscription-class and activity changes wake idle receives through cancellation generations. Final idle expiry checks committed hub membership and latest socket activity in the same statement as registration removal, because an asynchronous class notification can lag a successful subscription, instead of closing solely from the receiver's cached class ([lifecycle implementation](../../research/experiments/pubsub/README.md#implementation-boundaries)).
