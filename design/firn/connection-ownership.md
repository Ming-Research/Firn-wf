Decision: A connection keeps Firn's existing receive/execute/send owner until its first successful subscription, then transfers once to independent receive, command and output owners, because unsolicited delivery and EOF must proceed while commands or socket sends wait and never-subscribed GET/SET must retain their existing shared targets, instead of splitting every connection at acceptance ([ownership and delivery](../../research/investigations/firn/connection-waits.md#ownership-and-delivery)).

Decision: Membership transitions and complete reply groups commit under one event hub, and one non-network dispatcher publishes committed frames in order to independent per-connection output inboxes, because publication order must survive wake races while a slow socket cannot delay other subscribers, instead of publishers sending directly or racing to append to recipient inboxes after releasing the registry hold. Immutable publication bodies are retained once and shared by recipient frames; the stream has one output owner, including its partial-send offset ([atomic publication](../../research/investigations/firn/connection-waits.md#atomic-publication-not-a-wake-race)).

Decision: An admitted dataset command on a promoted connection holds the whole keyspace, metadata and event hub through execution and complete reply commitment, including data-only EXEC and declared-key scripts, because another client's observation of its effects must order a later publication after that reply and the existing whole-map command parts supply that protected execution boundary, instead of committing after releasing the data hold, reserving a reply position before data execution or extending every ordinary entry-handler interface with output commitment. All admitted promoted INFO forms also commit their complete reply under the dataset/metadata/hub boundary, with selected script-registry and server-state snapshots in that same non-waiting statement, because a key-count snapshot released before reply commitment permits a later SET/PUBLISH transaction to precede an INFO reply that reports the earlier empty database; host heap and residency samples precede the statement, and ordinary INFO retains its original snapshot path ([INFO observation boundary](../../research/investigations/firn/connection-waits.md#promoted-info-observation-and-reply-ordering)). Standalone PING, ECHO, TIME, SELECT, COMMAND and OBJECT HELP retain ordinary handlers and the promoted owner's later reply commitment, because they observe no dataset state; a publication independent of those controls may enter output order before or after their replies. Controls nested in EXEC or scripts remain within the enclosing execution-unit boundary. This broader promoted dataset hold has unmeasured contention cost; pre-command eviction retains its separate admission boundary. The shared admission coordinator and evaluator select ordinary or promoted execution by compile-time const instantiation, because the never-subscribed path must retain its original handlers and shared targets without a runtime promotion predicate or wrapper call ([visibility and reply ordering](../../research/investigations/firn/connection-waits.md#promoted-command-visibility-and-reply-ordering)).

Decision: A promoted connection charges the reserved byte capacities of each outstanding frame's prefix and body until that complete frame is released, charging a shared body in full to every recipient for its output limit, because a wire-length counter can miss retained spare capacity and must not lose the charge when ownership moves through hub, dispatcher, inbox and socket owner, instead of charging only enqueued wire bytes or releasing charges on dispatch. Active pub/sub clients use a hard threshold of at least 32 MiB and a soft threshold of at least 8 MiB continuously for more than 60 observed Unix seconds; normal clients have disabled output limits. Allocator rounding, queue storage and reference-control overhead are not represented by this charge, so closure at an identical publication count to Redis is not promised ([accounting and remaining evidence](../../research/investigations/firn/connection-waits.md#pubsub-output-accounting)).

Decision: Receive/send failure and pressure removal invalidate membership before cancellation wakes the independent owners, while QUIT and protocol errors commit an ordered finish marker after their reply, because cancellation, accepted command effects, reply delivery and child completion are distinct boundaries, instead of rolling back a completed command on a network error or joining a child before enabling its cleanup. A promoted command begins at its live-registration and cancellation admission check; invalidation prevents later admission while an already admitted command may finish, because queued cancellation can lag registry removal. Query bytes remain charged across both the receiver queue and parser's incomplete input, and closure occurs above one GiB; subscription-class and activity changes wake idle receives through cancellation generations. Final idle expiry checks committed hub membership and latest socket activity in the same statement as registration removal, because an asynchronous class notification can lag a successful subscription, instead of closing solely from the receiver's cached class ([lifecycle implementation](../../research/investigations/firn/connection-waits.md#pubsub-connection-ownership)).

- Reserving a reply position before acquiring the data hold: rejected because a GET can then observe a preceding SET/PUBLISH transaction while its reserved reply incorrectly precedes that transaction's publication ([counterhistory](../../research/investigations/firn/connection-waits.md#promoted-command-visibility-and-reply-ordering)).
